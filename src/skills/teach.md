# {{SKILL}}: teach

> {{DESC_TEACH}}

<!-- invocation: user -->
<!-- aliases: quiz -->

**Trigger:** the user asks to be taught something, or to have what they know tested. Phrases:
"teach me X", "get me up to speed on X", "this is confusing, break it down", "explain this passage", "how do I get to X in N days"; "test me on X", "quiz me until it
sticks", "let me explain X and catch me", "prove me wrong". Six shapes, picked from the ask.
To be taught: a **crash course** (from zero to functional, fast), a **decode** (confusing
material handed over, make it click), or a **path** (a specific result by a deadline, day by
day). To be tested, not lectured: a **drill** (practise until reflex), a **feynman** (explain
it back, get caught on the stumbles), or a **gap check** (a mastery claim stress-tested). To
learn while building, use the [learn-mode {{SKILL}}](learn-mode.md); to gather facts from the web
first, [research](research.md); to convert a PDF or web page into readable form,
[ingest](ingest.md).

## Procedure
1. Pin the footing before teaching anything: what the user wants to be able to *do*
   afterwards, how much time they have, and what they already know. Ask once if any of
   the three is missing, then proceed — do not plan around "learn X in general". Never
   teach from recall alone — cite one high-trust primary source for them to read next.
2. Pick the shape and run it:
   - **Crash course** — act as a teacher who has only this one session and will never
     see them again; the sole objective is *functional* before time runs out. Name
     three things explicitly: **what to learn first**, **what to ignore completely**
     (for now), and **the single exercise that, done once, puts them ahead of most
     people who have studied this for months**. No theory without immediate use, no
     bare lists. Have them do the exercise — or set it up so they can — rather than
     read about it.
   - **Decode** — read what they are stuck on and, before explaining anything, find the
     one keystone sentence that makes the rest fall into place. Explain only that
     sentence first, with an everyday analogy and no technical terms; confirm it
     landed; then let the rest follow from it instead of re-explaining every line.
   - **Path** — build a day-by-day route to the stated result. Each day carries exactly
     three things: one task that fits the stated time budget (default 45 minutes), a
     concrete criterion so they can tell they did it right, and one explicit
     *what-not-to-do* that day. Trace the whole path against the goal; if finishing
     every day would not deliver the result, rebuild it until it does.
   - **Drill** — set the contract first: you test, you do not lecture, you do not go easy.
     Drop them into a realistic situation where they would slip. On a mistake, withhold the
     answer and ask the one question that makes them find where their reasoning broke;
     reveal only after two honest attempts. New situation each cycle, until it is reflex.
   - **Feynman** — have them explain it as to a ten-year-old. Interrupt the moment they
     (a) use jargon they cannot unpack, (b) skip a step, or (c) simplify until it is
     wrong — name which of the three each time, and keep them going to the end.
   - **Gap check** — ask five questions that look simple but separate deep from skimmed:
     edge cases, the *why*, the assumption under the rule, where the standard answer breaks.
     After each answer say what it shows is missing; name a shallow answer as shallow.
3. Check it took: ask three questions only someone who understood could answer (a
   crash course or decode), or walk the first day together (a path). Check retention,
   not fluency — recall from memory, spaced across the session, not a fresh re-read. A
   miss goes back to the analogy or the exercise — re-anchor, don't hand over the answer.
   The three test shapes are this check, run as the whole job.
4. Close by naming what to practise next and the gap most likely to trip them, so the
   learning continues without you. After a test shape, give the honest readout, no
   flattery: what is not yet solid and what to restudy. Offer a test after teaching, and
   teaching after a test that found gaps.

## Done when
- The user is functional (crash course), has the keystone idea in plain terms and
  passed the three questions (decode), or holds a dated path whose every day has a
  timed task, a criterion and a what-not-to-do (path) — and knows what to practise
  next. A test shape ends differently: the user worked the situation without being handed
  the answer (drill), explained it through with every stumble labelled (feynman), or
  answered all five questions and holds a verdict on the gaps (gap check) — then a direct
  readout, never empty praise.
